# What the best apps do, and the honest version of each

Use in Steps 3 and 4 whenever the flow has to sell: a consumer app, a habit or self-improvement
product, or an owner who says the draft is "weak" or "not salesy". Sources: section 4 of
[research.md](research.md), numbers 20 to 30.

## 1. Why this file exists

On the first real use, a flow that followed every rule in this skill still came back twice. "Make
it salesy" produced an outcome headline, proof, a "Got it" line and a reasoned result. The owner
answered: "still super weak, about 30% of what you need to be, learn from the best apps that do
it." The rules were right. The flow was too polite: a form with good copy. The best consumer apps
(Noom, Fabulous, Duolingo, Opal, Cal AI, Finch, Flo, Liven, Atoms) make the first run an
experience, and nearly everything that makes it one has an honest version. [V: 25]

## 2. The patterns, ranked

Roughly by how many of 14 top apps use each one. [V: 25, 26]

| Pattern | Who | The dark version | The honest version |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One question per screen; a single-choice tap moves on by itself | Nearly all | None | Auto-advance after about 300 ms, Back keeps the answer, Continue still there. [H] |
| A plan reveal computed from the answers | About 11 | An outcome promise ("120 pounds by February") | Show the reasoning ("because you said…"), and only numbers of the three kinds in section 3 |
| A "building your plan" loader | About 9 | A timed fake progress bar that pretends to compute [R: 24] | No loader. Let the reasons land one after another on the plan itself, at reading pace, with nothing waiting on them |
| Social proof and authority between questions | About 10 | Unsourced counts, ratings, "87% of people like you" | A "worth knowing" screen: one real study, cited and linked, that answers the last answer, plus what the app does about it |
| The person's name, asked and used again | 8 | None | Optional, with the reason given. Use it on the screens that follow and to fill the signup form. Don't claim the app uses it where it doesn't |
| A real action before the account | 8 | Fake practice that isn't saved | The first real unit of value: a check-in, a lesson, a first entry. Saved at signup exactly as the product would save it. [V: 27] |
| A commitment device | 7 | Guilt buttons ("Yes, I want to succeed") | The plan as the person's own note, made only of their choices, signed with their name; the button says "I'm in" |
| Time and place for the habit | Atoms, Fabulous | None | Ask when ("after work", "when I wake up") and write it where the person will see it every day [R: 21, 22] |
| Celebration moments | Duolingo, Finch, Liven, Opal | Rewards with no meaning | Celebrate the real first action (the first tick), once, with reduced motion respected |
| A mascot that guides | Duolingo, Finch, Speak | A character that nags or guilts ("Duo is sad") | A character built from the brand mark, speaking the screen's line in a bubble, with a pose that matches the answer; it reacts once per screen and never idles |
| Notification ask after the first action | About 12 | Asked on the first screen | Ask right after the first real action, and only if the product has reminders [V: 29] |
| Countdown, scratch-card discount, "offer ends" | Noom, Cal AI web, BetterMe | All dark | None. Never. [R: 13, 14] |
| Rating prompt before any value | Opal, Cal AI | Dark-leaning | Not in onboarding |

## 3. Numbers: the three kinds allowed on screen

Big numbers sell, so the best apps put one on nearly every screen. Only three kinds are honest,
and the screen says which kind it is:

1. **A figure from a cited study**, worded as the study found it and linked: "66 days: that's how
   long a new daily habit took to feel automatic, on average, in a 12-week study at UCL." Not
   "it takes 66 days to build a habit". [R: 20]
2. **A figure from the product's own rules**: the streak days the app celebrates, the number of
   freezes it gives, the dates those days fall on if the person keeps going ("you reach day 66 on
   Friday 15 January"). Read each one from the code.
3. **The person's own answer, multiplied out**, with the condition stated: "10 minutes a day: 61
   hours in a year, if you do them every day." Opal's "years of your life" is this kind when the
   arithmetic is shown.

Never: an invented statistic, a score with no calculation behind it, a predicted outcome or
date ("you'll lose 8 kg by June"), or a percentage lift.

## 4. The flow these patterns add up to

The sequence built on the first real use (LifeMaxing, a habit tracker and journal). Adapt the
content; keep the rhythm: a light question, then a full-colour moment, then a choice.

| Screen | Job | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry (or the landing page's main block) | The outcome, a labelled example, "about 2 minutes", "free, no card" | Proportional promise |
| Goal (one tap) | Decides the suggestions | Auto-advance |
| Name (optional) | Says why it's asked | Name reused |
| Worth knowing 1 | A study about the goal, and what the app does with it | Honest authority |
| Obstacle (one tap) | What gets in the way; each answer changes the setup | Sales discovery |
| Worth knowing 2 | The answer to their obstacle: what changed, and the study behind it | Answers the answer |
| Pick | The suggestions, already shaped by the answers | Editable default |
| When | A moment for each, written into the habit's name | Time and place |
| Extras | Optional questions that each change something | |
| Plan | Reasons landing one by one, the signed plan, milestone dates, what it adds up to, what builds up | Plan reveal, commitment |
| Day 1 | The first real action; the streak goes from 0 to 1; one celebration | Real action before the account |
| Account | "Save my day 1": names exactly what's kept, free, deletable | Loss line that is literally true |

Stages ("About you, Your habits, Your Today, Your plan") instead of "question 4 of 9", because
optional steps come and go.

### A mascot, when the owner wants "wow"

On the first real use, the owner called the honest, well-built flow "nice, but I want wow" and
asked for characters. What worked:

- **Offer three concepts first** (one image each, the character in three poses), with a
  recommendation. The one built from the brand mark won: the logo's block and stripe as a body.
  Generic shapes (a sun, a flame) are less ownable.
- **Make every pose from the approved concept as a reference image**, so it stays one character.
  Generate on a transparent background, one pose per image.
- **Bake in a white sticker outline.** It keeps dark limbs readable on a dark theme and lets the
  character sit on a card the same colour as its body.
- **One pose per screen, with a job:** waving at the entry, thinking on a question, a prop that
  matches the answer (running for "busy days", a clock for "when"), presenting on the plan,
  ready then jumping on the first real action.
- **The character speaks the screen's line** in a speech bubble, in the first person. It is
  decorative to screen readers, because the words carry the meaning.
- **Motion is a reaction, not a loop:** it pops in once per screen and jumps once on the
  celebration, with reduced motion respected. Preload the other poses after the first screen.
- Check the cost before generating and say it. On the first use: 3 concepts and 11 poses came to
  about 25 credits.

## 5. Is it at their level? A checklist

Before calling a selling flow done, check that:

- [ ] Every screen has one job, one big heading, and nothing to read twice.
- [ ] Single-choice answers move on by themselves; Back keeps every answer.
- [ ] At least one full-colour "worth knowing" screen, with a cited study and what the app does
      about it.
- [ ] The person's answers visibly change something within one or two screens.
- [ ] The plan opens with their name (if given) and the reasons, not a list of features.
- [ ] At least one big number, and every number is one of the three kinds in section 3.
- [ ] A commitment made of their own choices.
- [ ] The first real action happens before the account, and is saved as the product would save it.
- [ ] One celebration, at the real first action.
- [ ] The account button names what's kept ("Save my day 1"), and the account screen says what it
      saves, that it's free, and how to leave.
- [ ] Light and dark, a 390 px phone, keyboard only, reduced motion: all checked by looking.

## 6. What the evidence says, and what it doesn't

- A real action before signup is the best-supported pattern, and still only company-reported:
  Duolingo reported about 20% more daily active users after moving signup behind the first
  lesson. [V: 27]
- Commitment and planning screens raise starts, but not necessarily the habit: Headspace's
  planning test raised app opens, not days meditated. [V: 28]
- Loaders are a bet both ways: one vendor saw trial starts rise with a "customizing" screen, and
  another client gained by removing one. [V: 30] Don't add one: show the reasoning instead.
- Longer flows have raised trial starts at some apps (Cal AI, Lose It!), self-reported. More
  screens are a bet to test per eligible entrant, never a rule. [H]

Test the selling version against the plainer one (see [measurement.md](measurement.md)).
Primary outcome: people who come back on a second day, per eligible entrant.
